Where it fails

How we tested

We translated sentences from the FLORES+ dataset, which has a professional translation of every sentence in all five languages. A machine translation counts as bad when it is in the least accurate quarter for its language, measured against the professional version. The flagger was trained on one part of the data and tested on another part it never saw.

What this does not show

  • The "bad" label is automatic. It compares wording with one professional translation, so a correct translation phrased differently can be marked bad. That is why native speakers on our team rated a sample themselves.
  • FLORES+ sentences read like news. Business text is shorter and full of numbers, so we also test on 60 business strings of our own.
  • The flagger looks for signs of trouble. It cannot prove a translation is right, and a fluent but wrong sentence can slip through.
  • This is a prototype. Translations of medical, legal or safety text should always be checked by a qualified person.
